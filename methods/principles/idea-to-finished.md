# From a raw idea to a finished thing

The same pipeline for a scene, a short video, a landing page or a product screen.
Steps 1-5 are thinking; the owner sees the result at step 8.

```
capture ─► sort ─► research ─► philosophy ─► plan + critic ─► build once ─► check as the viewer ─► review page ─► marks ─► rules
(verbatim) (who     (papers,    (the one      (5-10 lines,     (real          (phone size,           (pick buttons   (log)    (hard rules,
           answers) references, idea, rules)  outside eye)     quality)       knows nothing)         post comments)            method snapshot)
                    teardowns)
```

## 1. Capture the idea whole

- Save the owner's words **verbatim** (voice notes included), with date and time.
- Turn them into a **nuance table**: one row per nuance, and where it will live in
  the build. A transcribed voice brief loses its small words first; the table is
  how they survive.
- Keep the unusual part. Ideas that look odd are often what makes the product
  stand out; flattening them makes it generic.

## 2. Sort the open questions

Owner knows / owner's taste / research can answer (`evidence-first.md`). Only the
first two ever reach the owner.

## 3. Research what makes this kind of thing good

- Find the best real examples (by real views or real use, not by our taste), and
  study **why** they work: layout, timing in milliseconds, sound, what they leave
  out (`../research/reference-analysis.md`).
- When feel matters, **measure it**: record the reference, split it into frames, fit
  the curves (`../motion-graphics/measured-teardown.md`).
- Take the principle, never the art. A copy can't surpass the original.

## 4. Give it a philosophy

One idea, its rules and what it must never do (`product-philosophy.md`). For a
film: the one thing it teaches or proves. For a page: its one job.

## 5. Plan, then a critic pass

The 8-line plan and the critic questions in `running-the-work.md`. Sound and
motion get specced per moment: what the moment does, the motion, the sound, the
haptic.

## 6. Build once, at the real quality

- Real material and real voices from the start. Rough work shown to the owner
  reads as the product's quality.
- **One shared source** for every place the thing appears: the same scene file
  feeds the product and the films; the same player renders the page and the video
  frames (`../design/scene-kit.md`).
- Time-driven players, so a recorder gets exact frames (choppy video came from
  capturing at one frame rate and saving at another).

## 7. Check as the viewer

Before anyone sees it: watch or click every beat at phone size, as someone who
knows nothing about it. What are they looking at, and is it the thing that
matters? Screenshot every variation at phone width; broken variations are not
shown.

## 8. Show it on a review page

`../design/show-changes.md` and `skills/review-picker`. In short: a real thing
per item with one plain sentence ("This is X. You are rating Y."), one mark per
thing, pick buttons that post real comments, a comment button that opens the
page's own comment box, a bias line, phone size first.

## 9. Turn marks into rules

Log every mark to the preferences file, act on every comment, clear the page. A
repeated miss becomes a hard rule; a big change to a method gets a dated snapshot.
