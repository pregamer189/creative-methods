# Handoffs and replies

How finished work reaches a busy owner, and how replies are written, so they can
act in seconds and nothing gets lost. Collected from the owner's corrections in
November 2026. Planning and deciding live in `running-the-work.md`; review pages
in `../design/show-changes.md` and `skills/review-picker`.

## Replies

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **Lead with the result or the ask**, in one or two plain sentences | The owner skims between other work |
| **Always send the link** to the page or file the reply is about, in the same message | A result they have to hunt for is a result they don't see (Elias, 2026-11-09) |
| **Text meant to be pasted comes alone in a code block**, nothing around it. A second question gets one or two sentences at most | Copying around prose wastes a step and invites mistakes (Elias, 2026-11-11) |
| **Verdicts are icons**: a green check (good), an orange "!" (fix first), a red X (no) | Read at a glance, the same everywhere: replies, logs, review pages |
| **Do only what was asked.** No extra pieces, pages or features the owner didn't request; name a good extra idea in one line instead | Unasked additions cost review time and blur what changed (Elias, 2026-11-09) |
| **No tiny questions.** Research and decide; batch the real taste questions | See "Decide and log" in `running-the-work.md` |

## One item at a time

- **Hand over one finished item at a time**, the one the owner is about to use,
  with its file name and the exact text that goes with it (caption, title, alt
  text). Never a folder of ten for them to sort.
- **Only that item goes where the owner will grab it** (their phone's photos, a
  shared drop folder). The rest wait in a queue the owner can see.
- **A queue file says what is next**, with a date per item, so a fresh session can
  pick up the next item without asking.
- **Media is viewable in one tap**, inside the page or log the owner already
  uses, with no approval prompt or download step in the way.

## The log is always current

- **One running log per output stream** (posts, releases, sent items): what it is,
  when it goes out, its status, its results.
- **Every change goes into the log in the same turn**, never "later". Rejected
  items stay in the log marked as dropped, so they are never re-proposed.
- **Check "next up" every time** the log is touched; a stale "next" is the most
  common way a day gets skipped.

## Saved, parked and standing

| The owner says | What we do |
|---|---|
| "Save this, don't use it yet" | Save the link or words verbatim with the date in a "for later" list. Don't build, don't analyse beyond what was asked |
| "Park this" or "too early" | Keep the plan file as it is, write one line on why it was parked and what would unpark it, and move to what they named as the priority |
| "Yes to X from now on" (a standing approval) | Write it down where every session reads it, with the date and its exact scope. It covers that kind of step only, nothing wider |
| A hard rule ("never", "always") | Write it with their words, the date and the reason in the one home for rules, and check every later piece against it |

## Never-use lists

When the owner rejects a specific asset (a sound, a font, a stock clip, a phrase),
add it to a **never-use list** with their words and the date. Read the list
before picking any asset of that kind. A rejected asset coming back is the
fastest way to lose trust.

## Results, not opinions

- **Log real numbers per published piece** (views, average watch, follows, saves,
  replies) next to the piece in the log, a day after it goes out.
- **Compare formats by their numbers**, not by how they felt to make. When a
  format underperforms and the owner agrees, retire it and say which format
  replaces it.
- **The first second decides.** If average watch is under two seconds, fix the
  opening before anything else.
