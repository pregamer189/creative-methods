# Evidence first: build around what actually works

A standing principle from Elias (2026-11-08): "Because it is so data driven, it's
even more important that everything is built around what actually works." It
comes before taste, and it applies to every plan, design and build.

## Six rules

**1. Sort every open question before acting on it.** Three kinds:

| Kind | Examples | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| **The owner will know** | Facts about the owner, their money, time, accounts, partners | Ask once, in a batch of a few questions |
| **The owner's preference** | Taste, look, sound, feel | Show finished variations; the owner marks them |
| **Research can answer it, or get closer** | How people behave, what works, what platforms allow | Look it up (papers, the web, our own data and simulations), decide, log the reason. Never ask the owner |

Keep the live sort in one file per product. A plan that has not sorted its
questions is not ready.

**2. Variations come after understanding.** First the research and the data, then
the options the research leaves open, then variations of those. A page of ten
random variations pushes the design work onto the person marking it.

**3. Doing beats watching.** People get good at what the product helps with by
doing it themselves, in varied real situations, not by tapping through. Shortcuts
that make the first steps easy are fine, but they are a starting step, not the
goal, and they count for less.

**4. Show how it works; don't lecture.** Let people see how something works
inside a real example they already understand, with the part that matters made
visible. Brief, well-timed pointing helps; long explanations before use don't.
So: one plain line of what it does, and a "Why?" on demand. No jargon on the
main path.

**5. Prove the person can use it.** Every feature names the real outcome it should
move, measured in a new situation days later, not by finished steps. Time spent,
taps and streak length are never the success number. Measure drop-off step by
step through the core loop.

**6. Rescue what doesn't work, differently.** When something keeps not working
for a person (a step they fail, a feature they never get), it comes back **more
often and in a different form**, never as the same failed form again:

1. *More often:* bring it back sooner, until it works on a few separate days.
2. *Differently:* change one thing per return, in a fixed order, so you learn
   which change helps (context first, then presenter, then a picture, and only
   last a tip).
3. *Check for a mix-up:* if it always fails the same way, find what it is being
   confused with and separate the two.
4. *No failure feeling:* never label the person or the thing as weak or failed.
   It just comes back fresh.
5. *Measure the rescue:* the share that starts working within 30 days. If a form
   of return doesn't help, change the order.

Thresholds (how many misses, how many days) start as marked guesses and are tuned
on real data.

## How to apply

- Before writing a plan: list its open questions and sort them (rule 1).
- Every choice in a plan cites its evidence next to it; guesses are marked
  `[inferred]`.
- Every design review asks three things: does it make the person do the thing
  themselves, does it show structure without lecturing, and can we measure that
  they can use it?
- Every new feature names the outcome from rule 5 that it should move.
