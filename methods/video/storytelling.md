# Storytelling for short films

What makes a short product or maker film work, before any shot is planned.

## The core (Elias, I11 and I34, 2026-10-26)

- **One idea per film**, said in one sentence. Many ideas loosely joined is slop.
- **A film is a trust arc**, not a pile of likeable things. Pick the one step this
  film moves the viewer: **curiosity** (stop scrolling) → **understanding** (what it
  is) → **belief** (it really works, for someone like me) → **action** (try it).
- Show the features people actually care about, proven on a real screen.
- **Sell the value the viewer gets**, not the feature list (I36). Name the viewer's
  motive and the one behaviour principle the film leans on (curiosity gap, social
  proof, a visible before and after); persuasion is designed, not hoped for (I10).
  Never guilt or fake urgency.
- **Each product has its own audience and voice** (I35). Write for that audience;
  never reuse another product's tone.

## A story needs three things

(Andrew, "how to FILM Cinematic Videos SOLO", 2026. His order of importance:
story > shot list > shot order > light > edit > colour > thumbnail; viewers
criticise sound hardest.)

Design rule under all of it: if the viewer has to think about how to read the
frame, the design failed (Satori, design psychology). Shapes carry meaning by
tendency, not law: circles feel safe, squares stable, triangles and right arrows
progress, hexagons connection (Satori, shape psychology).

1. **Structure:** beginning, turn, end.
2. **A subject:** the maker, the user, or the product.
3. **Development:** something changes between the start and the end.

Write what you know. The part of your own story you find boring is often what
others find interesting.

## Five lines first

Situation, desire, conflict, change, result. If the film can't be told in five lines it
isn't clear yet; every later choice must connect back to one of them, and they expand
to ten or twenty without changing the core (Tim Runia, 1.3M views).

## Writing it

(Kallaway, by his own account a billion views; written for creators' short and long
videos, so it fits education and maker films better than cinema.)

- **Reality must beat expectation.** That's the mechanism under every rule here.
- **Order:** idea and title, outline, intro, body, ending. The title's promise is met
  in the first line (click confirmation).
- **Outline first, and gut-check it:** is any point actually new? If not, research more
  before writing. Summaries of what everyone knows lose people.
- **Second-best point first, best second, then the rest.** Rising value builds a
  pattern people stay for; falling value teaches them to leave.
- **Each point:** what it is, how to use it, why it fits the whole.
- **Re-hook between points:** a line that makes the next one necessary.
- **End on a high note:** a real last lift, like the chocolate with the bill.
- **Give every frame the "why":** memory forms where the viewer has something small to
  resolve (Satori). Predict what they will feel a few seconds later, not just now.

## Devices seen in the great ones

| Device | How | Seen in |
|---|---|---|
| The film does what it teaches | Open with the technique, then name it: "what I just did right there", "you're still here, aren't you?" | Andrew, *Simple Editing* (1.1k likes on that line) |
| A voice asks, the film answers | "But how?" lines pull the viewer to the next section | Andrew; Ermia Ramez as a whole conversation; Gawx's Q&A |
| Words leave, picture carries | The climax runs on music and image | Ermia Ramez, 6:50-9:38 |
| Show the way it was made | A process part with honest failures gets comments and trust | Andrew's behind the scenes; Kurzgesagt's meta video; Design Theory on visible effort |
| A name for those who stayed | "Part of the 20%": viewers comment it, and it reads as belonging | Andrew's top comments. Use only when true, and only once |
| Three chapters | Each a visual sequence paired with its own music | Andrew's closing advice |
| Real voices of the masters | Short archive clips of people who felt the same | Ermia Ramez |

## A series that opens a question

"Day 41: packing my dad's lunch until he rates one a 10/10." One word, *until*, opens a
gap that earns the follow as well as the watch: a rating sets an expectation for this
film, and "until" for the whole series (Amelia Willingham's breakdown, De2nFEHx4Uy, 15M
views on the original). The day count is also what Ermia's film ends on.

## Shapes that work

| Shape | Beats | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Framing, demo, closing | Problem in one line, the product doing it, one clear action | Product launch (30-45 s) |
| Before and after | The old way, the turn, the new way | A feature with a visible result |
| One-take demo | A real task, start to finish, no cuts that hide anything | Proof and belief |
| Maker story | Why I'm making this, one honest moment, what's next | Personal brand, build in public |
| Side by side | Same task two ways | Comparison with the usual way |
| Voiceover value film | A high-quality designed voice and distinct music carry it; short on-screen key lines, not full subtitles; it builds up to the value the viewer gets | Hype for a product (I36) |
| Animated story | A drawn or cut-out world explains one idea as a story (the Kurzgesagt kind of explainer); very hard, so storyboard every beat | "How it works" (I20) |

## The first 2 seconds

They must work muted: the real thing doing something, or a line of text that
lands. No logo first, no fade from black, no slow build.

## Made to be quoted (posting on X and similar)

Launches die at "congrats" (I43, from Juan, "How to Engineer Virality on X").
Give strangers something to add: a specific messy input → result → what you fixed
yourself. Plan several angles (maker story, implications, a reaction, a defensible
take, who gains and who loses), each for the people who'd care. Judge a film by
watch time, shares, saves, trials and messages, never by views alone.

## Order in a short reel

For reels under about 20 s: hook first, the ask second, the why third (Hormozi
reels, 2026-10). One strong line beats several. Comment-bait ("comment X") buys
reach, not trust: use it only when trust isn't the goal. A film about quality must
show that quality: viewers called a video on premium apps "AI slop" because it
wasn't (Tim Gabe comments).

## The ending

One clear action or the product name, on the music's last hit. No silent tail.
(Short reels may put the ask after the hook instead; see above.)

## Learn from great films, don't copy them

Take the *reason* a reference works (its pacing, its reveal, what viewers say they
loved), never its content (`../research/reference-analysis.md`). A copy can't
surpass its original (I13); doing what you see in great work, instead of what you
think would fit, can (I59).

## Check before storyboarding

- [ ] One sentence says the whole film.
- [ ] One trust step named.
- [ ] A turn exists: something changes.
- [ ] Every claim is true on a real screen today.
