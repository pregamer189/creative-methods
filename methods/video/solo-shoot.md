# Solo shoot (phone and gimbal)

How one person films a cinematic short alone. The storyboard says what to film;
this says how. Main source: Andrew, "how to FILM Cinematic Videos SOLO in 2026"
(full captions read), plus general phone-film practice.

## Shot list: wide, close, action

For each line of the story film three shots: **wide** (where are we), **close**
(how does the person feel), **action** (hands or object). Start wide, get tighter.
Mix angles. That's what turns a few seconds into a scene.

## Gimbal moves

| Move | How | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| Push in | Walk slowly toward the subject | Tension, a reveal |
| Pull out | Walk slowly back | Endings, showing the place |
| Follow | Low behind feet or hands | Moving forward |
| Orbit | Circle the subject, keep it centred | A key object |
| Reveal | Start behind a tree or wall, slide past | Openings |
| Locked | Gimbal still, subject moves | Calm, talking |

Walk heel to toe with bent knees. Hold 3 s before and after each move so the edit
has room. Three takes per shot.

## Camera

- 4K. Frame rate matched to the local mains: 25 fps where mains is 50 Hz (Europe),
  30 fps at 60 Hz, so lights don't flicker. Double it for slow motion.
- Lock focus and exposure on the subject before recording.
- Main 1x lens; zoom with your feet.
- Shoot vertical for vertical feeds; don't crop later.

## Light

- Outside: the hour before sunset; sun to the side or behind for edge light.
- Inside: one lamp as key light on one side of the face, a warm practical lamp in
  the background so the light makes sense. Contrast looks cinematic.

## Sound

- Voiceover in a small soft room (a wardrobe of clothes, under a duvet), phone
  about 15 cm from the mouth.
- 20 s of room tone at every location, plus the real sounds (steps, a door, a cup).
  Sound is half the feel.

## Expect waste

Andrew filmed four hours for 40 seconds. Try a shot; if it fails, change one thing
and try again. After each film, write one lesson down.
