# Short videos for a teaching account

A teaching account posts mostly **short teaching pieces in the product's niche**,
and only now and then something about the product. It earns trust by being useful
every day, before it asks for anything. This is a different kind of film from a
product reel: the hook-ask-why order in `storytelling.md` is for product reels;
teaching posts have no ask.

## The account

- **About four teaching posts to one product post.** No product interface at the
  bottom of teaching clips, nothing too interactive.
- **No call to action and no hook text** on teaching posts. Tiny captions, the
  subject shown once, calm.
- **Never pose as an expert you aren't.** Faceless and calm is fine; a "learning it
  myself while building it" arm is a separate test, the owner's call.
- **Never repeat a released piece.** Parts may come back inside a new piece; vary the
  format day to day from a small format library (video, swipe carousel), easy to
  harder.
- **Only checked facts.** Nothing an AI wrote unchecked goes on screen.
- **Post one at a time** (at the same hour, a couple of days apart) and compare
  finish rate at 48 hours. Short-video platforms have no organic A/B test, so tests
  run in sequence. Log the stats (views, average watch time, finish rate, the 0-3 s
  drop) in one table.
- Give the post text as one block that can be copied, with the account's fixed
  hashtag set.

## Ten principles for each video

1. **One thing per video.** One small, complete idea; everything else serves it.
   Videos have a red thread across days instead of cramming.
2. **The frame shows only what is being taught.** A thing enters the moment it is
   named and is marked while it is spoken. Nothing else moves or appears; a bird
   flying past while something else is taught steals the eye.
3. **Show before you ask.** A question comes only after each part has been seen
   and heard on its own.
4. **One claim, shown where you look.** Each explanation makes one true claim; the
   part lights up with its label right beside it, in the same colour. No treasure
   hunts.
5. **Grow out of context.** The detail scales up out of where it sits, the rest
   fades, and it shrinks back into place. One element, one movement, no cross-fade
   between separate things.
6. **Fast, clean transitions:** about 350-450 ms, ease-out. No beat longer than
   about 2.5 s unless a voice is speaking.
7. **One viewing has to be enough.** See it, hear it, see the detail, link one part,
   then hear it whole again. The last frame matches the first, so the video loops.
8. **Plain, kind copy.** Short and true. Never imply the viewer should already know
   ("So...", "Remember...").
9. **Quiet sound.** The voice, plus at most one very subtle sound: the sound of the
   arriving thing, timed to the line that hands it over. No background beds, no
   ticks.
10. **Check like a viewer before sending.** Watch every beat at phone size as someone
    who knows nothing: what are they looking at, and is it the thing being taught?

## Timing rules

- **No empty start or end** (Elias, 2026-11-08). The voice starts about **0.35 s** in
  (under half a second; 0.15 s felt choppy), and the cut comes about **0.35 s** after
  the last line, so the loop is tight.
- A pause for the viewer to think is about **4 s**, with a thin timer bar (1.5 s was
  too short).
- Never start a sound under a spoken word.

## Craft that worked

- **Act it out:** when the content allows, the things shown do what the voice says.
- **A second voice that answers** makes a scene a small exchange instead of a lecture.
- **A barely visible wave follows the voice** across the spoken line (a slight tone
  step or brightness, continuous, not per word), so the eye knows where the voice is.
- **Mouths follow loudness**, smoothly, with no delay.
- **Each caption sits under the line it belongs to**, not stacked at the bottom.
- **Soft tones for the fixed parts** of a pattern, so the structure is visible
  without being explained.
- **Frame-exact recording:** drive the page on virtual time and write every frame;
  recording at one rate and saving at another repeats frames and looks choppy.
- **Snapshot the method** each time it changes in a big way, so results can be
  traced to a version.
