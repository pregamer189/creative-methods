# Landing and waitlist pages

How we turn a spoken brief into a soft, high-quality page that people enjoy
scrolling, without it becoming loud. Brand values (art, type, colours, name, copy)
stay in the product; this is the method.

## Start from the brief, verbatim

Voice briefs lose their small words in transcription. Save the owner's words as they
were said, then write a **nuance table**: one row per nuance (N1, N2, ...) and where
it is visible in the build. The build is checked against the table, row by row.
Read it back to the owner in one short paragraph before building, so they know you
are on the right track.

## One page, two entries

- **The waitlist link** opens with the signup as the hero; the story sits below.
- **The landing link** opens on the story; the hero has one key ("Here to join the
  waitlist?") that takes you to the signup at the bottom, with the journey playing
  fast on the way.
- Same page, an entry flag in the link. One source, no drift between two pages.

## The scroll is a journey

- Scrolling moves one continuous camera through places (say, from high up, down
  through layers, into somewhere new), each place telling one part of the story
  with one real example.
- **Film-like wipes** between places: something covers the frame very fast and
  clears (fog, a sheet of something, a playful wipe), about 350 ms of scroll. Make your own
  version of a wipe you admire; never copy its look.
- A change of direction is a strong beat: vertical scroll that suddenly drives a
  sideways ride feels like getting into a car.
- Depth by **stepped solid bands**, never gradients.

## Soft, with a lot happening, never overwhelming

- At most 2-3 moving things per screen, slow and damped; small background life
  (drifting clouds, a passing plane or boat).
- **Micro-interactions on tap:** tap a cloud, a boat, a character, and it answers.
  A small surprise near the signup (something peeking up when the field is focused).
- **No background sound.** Sound stays off until the visitor taps something, and then
  only short, real, liked sounds on taps.
- Minimal words: the one idea, a line per place, one real example each.
- Real lit art (`../design/scene-kit.md`); the product's own keys and type.
- **Reduced motion:** wipes become plain cuts, nothing drifts.

## Signup

- Stub the form (nothing stored or sent) until the email service and domain are
  chosen, and say so on the review page only.
- One field and one key; confirmation is a real moment, not a toast.

## Review

Phone size first. Show variations only of parts the brief left open (hero, signup
block, wipes, background motion), each on its own card with "This is X. You are
rating Y."; Like / Don't like / Neither post real comments.
